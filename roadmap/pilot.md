# Pilot and demand validation

**Proposed experiment, not evidence of demand.** Recruit a small group with recurring information or creative work and a willingness to show their existing workflow. The selected universal-workspace strategy remains intact; a focused pilot reduces the number of things that must work at once.

## Recruitment and consent

Begin with founder-led conversations and an invited catalog. Ask participants to bring a real recent task, explain how they completed it, and identify the result they would consider useful. Do not count curiosity, a waitlist registration or a friendly interview as demand. Record task frequency, alternatives and switching friction without collecting unnecessary sensitive data.

A proposed starting cohort is 8–12 invited users, chosen for learning rather than statistical significance. Confirm whether they consent to task observation and minimal analytics. Explain supported apps, external providers, retention, costs and support availability before use. Exclude regulated or high-consequence workflows, unattended external writes and sensitive production datasets until appropriate controls are demonstrated.

## Experiment sequence

| Question | Method | Evidence | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Does one app solve a real job? | Participant uses their own task with a predefined output rubric | Completed output, participant acceptance, time/cost and founder interventions | Repair task fit before adding apps |
| Will they return? | Observe a second naturally occurring task over two task cycles | Voluntary return and another useful result | Narrow audience or revise job if use depends on reminders |
| Does shared context help? | Counterbalanced comparable tasks with and without allowed shared context | Less repeated input or higher quality, with no disclosure/permission failure | Keep cross-app claim hypothetical if no improvement |
| Will they pay? | Present a clear real offer after payment safety is verified | Completed payment, refund/cancellation and continued use | Separate willingness statements from money collected |
| Can economics work? | Reconcile each task’s full variable cost | Collected revenue less provider, compute, storage, fees and manual support | Cap or retire unviable workflows |

## Bias and interpretation

Do not infer retention from founder-assisted demos. Tag every assisted session; report assisted and independent outcomes separately. A small cohort supports qualitative decisions, not population-level claims. Report counts and denominators, missing events, observation windows and reasons for withdrawal.

Repeated usage can reflect novelty or subsidies. Follow at least two natural task cycles and test a transparent price after value is established. Never show fake checkout success or simulated purchases as payment evidence. A payment sandbox is technical verification only.

## Exit and stop criteria

Use the explicitly [proposed thresholds](metrics.md) as a starting discussion, with pre-registered definitions before recruitment. Stop exposure immediately on isolation or uncontrolled-spend failure. Narrow the bundle if users cannot complete core tasks independently. Reconsider positioning if participants get useful results but still prefer their existing tools at the offered price.

Suggested accountability: product/research owner records evidence; engineering owner owns defects; operations owner owns access and incident response. These may be one person, and capacity must be made explicit before admitting a cohort.
