# Users and jobs to be done

**Proposed product decision · 17 October 2026.** Recruit an invited pilot of independent creators and small project teams who repeatedly gather material, form a brief, and create something from it. This is an entry point into the universal workspace, not a vertical customer-feedback product. No customer count or existing adoption is asserted.

## Choose people by recurring work

| Participant | Job and current workaround to investigate | Value hypothesis | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent researcher or creator | “When a project changes, help me turn scattered sources into an inspectable brief without rebuilding context.” Uses assistant chats, notes, and copied links | Reusable source context and consistent outputs | Last real project, repeated setup steps, factual errors, accepted output |
| Small project collaborator | “When I receive a colleague's brief, let me adapt it without inheriting their private accounts or losing its origin.” Uses shared documents and chat | Explicit audience, provenance, and safe reuse | Actual handoff, permission understanding, time spent repairing context |
| Technical maker | “When I automate a recurring job, let me ship a usable application without building another account and billing system.” Uses scripts, workflow builders, or standalone apps | Shared services and a path to distribution | Independent install, first run, upgrade, support burden |
| Workspace operator | “When an execution fails or becomes costly, let me stop it, explain the charge, and restore service.” Uses logs and manual support | Bounded execution and accountable costs | Incident rehearsal, reconciliation, access revocation |

The person using an application, the person paying for a workspace, and the person publishing an application may differ. Record each role during interviews. An enthusiastic developer is not evidence that end users will pay; a satisfied user is not evidence of publisher economics.

## First-release exclusions

Do not recruit workloads requiring clinical, legal, credit, or other consequential decisions without specialist governance. Avoid regulated-data promises, guaranteed residency, enterprise procurement commitments, or business-critical unattended workflows before the operational evidence exists. Exclude open anonymous publication and arbitrary untrusted execution from the pilot contract.

Large enterprises seeking replacement of an established automation estate are a poor first validation group: their procurement, identity, and integration requirements can overwhelm the workspace question. General consumers with no recurring job can test usability, but cannot establish repeat-job value by completing a novelty demo.

## Interview around a completed job

Ask participants to bring a recent task and the sources they are entitled to share. Establish the baseline before demonstrating IntoChat: tools used, active effort, elapsed time, failure points, quality standard, and paid alternatives. Observe one task without coaching beyond onboarding. Record every intervention so founder-assisted success is not reported as independent activation.

Repeat the job when it naturally recurs. Ask what the participant actually used in between, whether they exported the result, and whether they would lose anything meaningful if the workspace disappeared. A favorable survey is weaker evidence than unsolicited reuse on a new task.

## Activation and retention semantics

**Proposed definitions:** activation is an eligible invited user completing a useful application outcome and saving or exporting it, with the permission and cost explanation understood. Report the denominator as eligible users who started onboarding, alongside invitations and non-starters. A login, installed app, or model reply is not activation.

Repeat usage is a useful outcome on a separate real task after the first session. Report cohorts by activation date and opportunity to repeat; a monthly job should not be judged only by daily activity. Cross-application use requires an intentional handoff of a real artifact or context object that helps a second outcome. Accidental navigation between two apps does not count.

Use pseudonymous identifiers and minimize captured source content. Evaluation consent is separate from permission to read material during task execution. See [experience](./experience), [hypotheses](./hypotheses), and [pricing](./pricing) for outcome, evidence, and payment boundaries.
